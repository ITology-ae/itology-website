Supply / Routes / Continuity

When a route changes, value depends on what you can see next.

Hormuz is a visible reminder that energy resilience is not only a shipping question. It is a connected decision across maritime trade, pipelines, land transport, ports, terminals, storage, suppliers, insurance, OT/IT, and production commitments.

Baseline Operating State

Routes are available, dependencies are easy to underestimate.

Sea lanes, pipelines, ports, roads, rail, storage, terminals, and digital controls appear stable — while concentration risk and unused alternatives remain hidden.

Disruption-Exposed State

A route change becomes an enterprise decision.

Rerouting changes lead times, capacity, insurance exposure, storage needs, port constraints, operating cost, and the consequences of delayed supply.

ITology Decision Layer

Compare options before the disruption decides for you.

Map dependencies, test multimodal alternatives, quantify loss and recovery, and prioritize actions across sea, land, pipeline, asset, and OT/IT layers.
